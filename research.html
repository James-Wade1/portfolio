Reliable and Adaptive World Models for Manipulation

Autonomous Robotic Manipulation (ARM) Lab

September 2026 – Present · Graduate Research Assistant

A robot can plan with a learned model of the world. What happens when the physics change? I study how generative world models can adapt to new physical conditions and how to identify model errors before they lead to poor decisions.

  • Self-supervised adaptation: Ran fine-tuning experiments that adapt a world model using its own estimates instead of ground-truth rewards.
  • Comparative evaluation: Benchmarked adaptation against offline reinforcement learning and imitation-learning baselines.
  • Reliability at planning time: Developing learned estimators of world-model error and studying their ability to detect out-of-distribution dynamics compared with likelihood-based signals.

Fault-Tolerant, Rigidity-Preserving Control of Inflatable Truss Robots

Compliant Mechanisms and Robotics Group (CMR) | Brigham Young University

Duration: August 2023 – August 2026 | Role: Undergraduate Researcher | Funding: Utah NASA Space Grant ($2,000)

Developed control methods that keep inflatable truss robots structurally rigid and mobile after actuator failures, preserving over 62% of nominal workspace and reducing tracking RMSE by over 22%.

  • Algorithm Development: Formulated discrete-time control barrier function constraints on rigidity singular values to preserve structural rigidity under actuator failures
  • Validation: Developed failure-aware kinematics, state estimation, and encoder-feedback control
  • Tools & Technologies: Python, discrete-time CBFs, kinematic modeling, embedded control systems
