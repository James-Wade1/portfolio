Trebuchet Simulation with Lagrange Multipliers

Graduate-level Dynamics | Brigham Young University | 2025

As part of my graduate dynamics coursework (ME 534) at BYU, I developed a comprehensive simulation of a trebuchet using Lagrangian mechanics and constraint forces. The trebuchet represents an interesting dynamic system that transitions through three distinct phases of motion: sliding, swinging, and flying. Each phase presents unique kinematic constraints that must be properly enforced through Lagrange multipliers.

The simulation was implemented using SymPy for symbolic derivation of the equations of motion, which allowed for easy parameter adjustments and ensured mathematical accuracy. These symbolic expressions were then converted to NumPy functions for efficient numerical integration using SciPy's solve_ivp. The results were visualized using Matplotlib to create an animated replay of the trebuchet motion.

Theoretical Framework

Euler-Lagrange Equations

The dynamics of the trebuchet system are derived using the Euler-Lagrange equations with constraints. For a system with generalized coordinates \(\mathbf{q}\) and Lagrangian \(L = T - V\) (kinetic energy minus potential energy), the equations of motion take the form:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{\mathbf{q}}}\right) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{q}} = \mathbf{Q} + \mathbf{a}^T\boldsymbol{\lambda}$$

where \(\mathbf{Q}\) represents non-conservative generalized forces, \(\mathbf{a}\) is the constraint coefficient matrix, and \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\) is the vector of Lagrange multipliers representing constraint forces. This formulation can be rewritten in matrix form as:

$$\mathbf{M}\ddot{\mathbf{q}} = \mathbf{F} + \mathbf{a}^T\boldsymbol{\lambda}$$

where \(\mathbf{M}\) is the mass matrix and \(\mathbf{F}\) contains all applied and non-conservative forces including gravity and friction.

Kinematic Constraints

The trebuchet system is defined by four generalized coordinates:

$$\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x & y & \theta & \varphi \end{bmatrix}^T$$

where \(x\) and \(y\) represent the horizontal and vertical position of the projectile, \(\theta\) is the angle of the main arm, and \(\varphi\) is the angle of the sling relative to the arm.

The constraints vary by phase. During the sliding phase, two constraints enforce that the projectile remains on the ground (\(y = h\)) and attached to the arm at distance \(l\). During the swinging phase, only the attachment constraint remains active as the projectile has lifted off the ground. In the flying phase, all constraints are released and the projectile follows ballistic motion.

Lagrange Multipliers for Constraint Forces

The Lagrange multipliers \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\) directly represent the magnitudes of constraint forces. To solve for these forces, we combine the equations of motion with the constraint acceleration equations:

$$\mathbf{a}\ddot{\mathbf{q}} + \dot{\mathbf{a}}\dot{\mathbf{q}} = \mathbf{0}$$

This system can be written in augmented form:

$$\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{M} & \mathbf{a}^T \\ \mathbf{a} & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \ddot{\mathbf{q}} \\ \boldsymbol{\lambda} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{F} \\ -\dot{\mathbf{a}}\dot{\mathbf{q}} \end{bmatrix}$$

Solving this linear system yields both the accelerations \(\ddot{\mathbf{q}}\) and the constraint forces \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\). For the trebuchet, \(\lambda_1\) is related to the tension force in the sling, while \(\lambda_2\) represents the normal force from the ground during the sliding phase.

Mass Matrix and Force Vector

The mass matrix for the trebuchet system, derived from the kinetic energy, is:

$$\mathbf{M} = \begin{bmatrix} m_P & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & m_P & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & I_{AO} + d_W^2 m_W & -d_G d_W m_W \sin(\varphi - \theta) \\ 0 & 0 & -d_G d_W m_W \sin(\varphi - \theta) & I_{WG} + d_G^2 m_W \end{bmatrix}$$

The generalized force vector includes gravitational effects, friction, and velocity-dependent terms:

$$\mathbf{F} = \begin{bmatrix} -\mu N \\ -g m_P \\ -d_A g m_A \cos(\theta) + d_G d_W m_W \cos(\varphi - \theta)\dot{\varphi}^2 + d_W g m_W \cos(\theta) \\ -d_G d_W m_W \cos(\varphi - \theta)\dot{\theta}^2 - d_G g m_W \sin(\varphi) \end{bmatrix}$$

Implementation Approach

The simulation was implemented in Python using a symbolic-to-numeric workflow. First, SymPy was used to symbolically define all position vectors, velocities, the Lagrangian, and constraint equations. This symbolic approach allowed for automatic differentiation and ensured mathematical correctness.

The symbolic expressions for M, F, a, and da/dt were then converted to highly optimized NumPy functions using SymPy's lambdify feature. This combination provided the flexibility of symbolic mathematics during development with the computational efficiency needed for numerical integration.

The system was integrated through time using SciPy's solve_ivp with automatic phase detection. When constraint forces indicated a phase transition (e.g., normal force approaching zero for liftoff, or tension becoming slack for release), the simulation would switch to the appropriate constraint set and continue integration.

Summary

This project demonstrated the power of Lagrangian mechanics with constraint forces for analyzing complex multi-phase dynamic systems. The symbolic-to-numeric workflow using SymPy and NumPy proved highly effective, enabling rapid parameter studies while maintaining mathematical rigor. The Lagrange multiplier approach elegantly handled the switching constraint topology across the three phases of motion, providing physically meaningful force calculations throughout the simulation. The animated visualization created with Matplotlib provided valuable insight into the energy transfer mechanisms that make the trebuchet an effective launching device.
